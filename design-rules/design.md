# Website Design Plan

---

## What This Website Is

This is a developer portfolio website. But it's not just a list of projects — it's meant to feel like a journey. When someone visits, they should feel like they are stepping into your story as a developer. By the time they reach the bottom of the page, they should feel confident enough to reach out and book a call to work with you.

---

## Who This Is For

**Main visitor:** Business owners who need something built and are looking for the right developer to hire.

**What they should do:** Book a call with you. Everything on this page should lead toward that one action.

---

## Page Layout (top to bottom)

### 1. Hero Section (top of the page)
The very first thing visitors see. This should immediately tell them who you are and what you do. It needs a short, punchy headline and a clear button that invites them to learn more or scroll down. This sets the tone for the whole journey.

### 2. Skills Section
A section that shows the technologies and tools you know. Think of this as "here's what I bring to the table." It should feel confident but not overwhelming. Simple icons or labels work well here — no long paragraphs.

### 3. Projects Section
A gallery of your past work. Each project should have an image they can click, a short title, and a brief description of what it is. This is where you prove you can actually build things.

### 4. Contact Section
A simple form at the bottom of the page where visitors can reach out. The form should ask for:
- Their name
- Their email address
- A short message (what they need help with)

There should also be a clear call-to-action near the form encouraging them to book a call.

---

## Look and Feel

**Overall vibe:** Clean, minimal, professional. Light backgrounds. Easy to read. Not cluttered. The kind of site that feels like it was made by someone who pays attention to detail.

**What to avoid:** Do not make this look like a generic AI-generated site. That means:
- No purple or violet gradients
- No glowing neon effects
- No blob shapes floating in the background
- No heavy dark backgrounds covered in glowing cards
- No overused "techy" visual tricks
- Keep it real, grounded, and human

---

## Colors

This site uses the 60-30-10 color rule:

| Role | Color | Where it's used |
|---|---|---|
| 60% — Background | Off-white (near white, slightly warm) | Most of the page background |
| 30% — Main text and structure | #4c061d (deep wine / dark maroon) | Headings, body text, navigation |
| 10% — Accent | #D17A22 (warm amber / burnt orange) | Buttons, links, highlights, hover effects |

This palette is warm, distinctive, and professional. It will stand out from the sea of blue-and-white developer portfolios without looking flashy.

---

## Interactive Features

**Project Gallery:** The projects section will have clickable images. When a visitor clicks on a project image, it should expand or show more detail. This lets you show your work without cluttering the page.

**Contact Form:** A simple form with three fields (name, email, message) and a send button. When submitted, it should give the visitor a confirmation that their message was received.

---

## Things to Avoid

- No purple or violet color schemes
- No glowing neon effects or neon-on-dark designs
- No floating blob or abstract shape backgrounds
- Nothing that makes the site feel cold, robotic, or AI-generated
- No stock photos of people in offices or hands typing on laptops
- Keep animations subtle — if anything moves, it should be gentle and purposeful

---

## Notes (decisions made on your behalf)

Since you're a beginner, a few small technical decisions were made for you:

- **Font:** A clean, readable font will be chosen that feels professional without being boring. No comic-style or overly decorative fonts.
- **Mobile friendly:** The site will automatically look good on phones and tablets, not just computers.
- **No outside tools needed:** Everything will be built from scratch — no third-party plugins or paid services required to make it work.
- **Booking a call:** Since you don't have a booking tool set up yet, the contact form will serve as the main way for business owners to reach you. A booking button can be added later once you have a link (like Calendly).
